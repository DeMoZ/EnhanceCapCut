## Dubbing in the speaker's own voice

**New: Clone the original voice.** In Subtitles & Voice-over, turn on **Clone the original voice** and the
voice-over speaks every translated line in the voice of the person who said it, with that line's intonation and
emotion: an excited line stays excited, a quiet one stays quiet. It runs on your computer with Chatterbox
Multilingual (Resemble AI, open source); nothing is uploaded.

- Each line is copied from the original speech at that line, so the delivery follows the video line by line.
  **Expressiveness** makes it calmer or more dramatic (0.5 sounds like the original).
- Every line is listened back with Whisper and made again when it came out garbled; lines that run long are sped up
  (at most 1.35×) without changing the pitch.
- Languages: Arabic, Danish, Dutch, English, Finnish, French, German, Greek, Hindi, Italian, Korean, Malay, Norwegian,
  Polish, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish, Swahili, Swedish, Turkish. Other languages are spoken by the chosen system
  voice.
- The model is downloaded once (1.55 GB, plus Whisper's 574 MB). A line takes about twice its length to make on an
  M1 Mac, so a 9-minute video takes 20 minutes or so; it's slower on a PC without a fast CPU.
- **Only clone your own voice or the voices of people who agreed to it.** The window asks you to confirm their
  consent before every run.
- Leave the box off and everything works as before, with system voices.

On Windows the voice is copied from the timeline's mix (there is no voice isolation there yet), so music under the
speech can color it a little.

## Voice-over goes to a new timeline

A voice-over run now adds a **new timeline** to the project, a copy of the selected one named
`<project>_dub_<languages>_<original audio>_<n>` (e.g. `Trip_dub_en_muteall_1`), and leaves the original timeline
as it was. Run it again with other choices and you get another timeline to compare. Subtitles on their own still go
into the selected timeline.

## More choices for the original sound

What happens to the project's own sound while the voice-over speaks:

- **Replace voice** (Mac): the original voice disappears, the music and ambience stay. The sound is muted under the
  voice-over and a track with the same sound minus the voice plays in its place, cross-faded.
- **Remove vocal** (Mac and Windows): turns on CapCut's own "Remove vocal" on the clips under the voice-over, so
  CapCut separates them itself. Clips it can't separate (compound clips, CapCut music, reversed clips, sources over
  10 minutes) are muted instead.
- **Mute** is now called **Mute all**; **Keep** and **Lower** are unchanged.

## Command line

- `ecc subtitles … --voice en=clone --consent` dubs in the cloned voice (`--exaggeration 0.5` sets the
  expressiveness); `--original replace` (Mac) and `--original remove` are the new sound options.
- `ecc clone reference.wav --language en --text "…" -o out.wav` tries voice cloning on a single sentence.

## Downloads

- `EnhanceCapCut-0.8.0.pkg` — macOS app (Apple Silicon and Intel), installs into /Applications.
- `EnhanceCapCut-0.8.0-windows-x64.zip` — Windows app `EnhanceCapCut.exe` and command-line tool `ecc.exe`; unzip
  anywhere, no .NET or Windows App SDK install needed.

Both builds are unsigned: on macOS, allow the installer in System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security; on Windows,
SmartScreen may ask to confirm. If you have 0.7.0 or later, **Check for Updates…** installs this version.
